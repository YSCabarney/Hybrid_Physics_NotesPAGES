Debate 1
The Chester
Intersection Crash
Team Velocity  vs.  Team Displacement
Debate 1 — What Happened

A car runs a red light at 9th & Avenue of the States

4:17 PM — A sedan heading north runs the light and collides with a delivery van heading east.

Speed limit: 25 mph ≈ 11.2 m/s
Skid marks before impact: 18.4 m
GPS logged position every second for 6 seconds before the crash
At t = 5–6, GPS shows the car drifted 2.1 m eastward — but the road goes straight north
The driver: GPS malfunction.   The insurance company: the data says otherwise.
Debate 1 — The Claims
Was the car speeding?
Side A — Not Speeding
Team Velocity
GPS shows constant 11.2 m/s for t = 0–4, exactly the speed limit. The 2.1 m drift is within GPS error range (±1.5 m) and doesn't prove real movement.
Side B — Was Speeding
Team Displacement
The 2.1 m drift exceeds the ±1.5 m GPS error threshold — that's real lateral movement. Displacement data plus skid marks show the car was over the limit.

Debate 2
The Disputed
Goal
Team Shot  vs.  Team Deflection
Debate 2 — What Happened

84th minute. Chester FC down 1–0. Shot, deflection, disputed call.

Midfielder shoots toward far post: 48 N at 32° east of north
Sliding defender makes contact simultaneously: 18 N at 15° west of north
Ball was stationary when both forces applied. Travels 14 m to the goal line in 1.8 s
Goal-line technology was offline. No definitive camera angle.
Goal width: 7.32 m · Ball starts 9 m left of center, 14 m out
The referee waved it off. Chester FC protested. The protest will be decided on the physics.
Debate 2 — The Claims
Did the ball cross the line?
Side A — Goal
Team Shot
Net force on the ball pointed inside the far post. The deflection was too weak to redirect it wide. The ball crossed the line.
Side B — No Goal
Team Deflection
The defender's force changed direction enough to push the ball outside the post. The net force vector points wide. It didn't cross.
