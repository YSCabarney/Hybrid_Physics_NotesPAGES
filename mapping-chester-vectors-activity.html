Mapping Chester: Vectors by Hand

You know a vector has a size and a direction. This week you'll learn to do math with that direction: split vectors apart, add them, and use a map to find how far you really are from Subaru Park.

Parts 1–5: learn the tools

⏱ about 70 min total

Each part starts with a worked example in the packet. Read it first, then try the problems. Here's what each part is for.

The one picture to remember

Every problem this week starts with a triangle like this. The vector is the hypotenuse. The components are the two legs.

Part 1 · Warm-up: Which way is that?10 min · no calculator

Directions like "30° N of E", the sign rules (east and north are +), and a prediction about walking around the block.

Watch out: "30° N of E" and "30° E of N" are not the same direction. Draw both to see why.

Part 2 · Two perpendicular vectors15 min

When two vectors meet at 90°, the resultant is the hypotenuse.

R = √(x² + y²)

Watch out: square each leg first, then add, then take the square root. √(3² + 4²) is not 3 + 4.

Part 3 · Breaking a vector apart (SOHCAHTOA)20 min

Sin = Opp ÷ Hyp · Cos = Adj ÷ Hyp · Tan = Opp ÷ Adj. When θ is measured from the east–west axis:

x = R cos θ     y = R sin θ

Watch out: if the angle is measured from north (like "25° W of N"), sine and cosine swap. The side next to the angle always uses cosine.

Part 4 · Adding vectors15 min

Draw: tip-to-tail, then the resultant goes from first tail to last tip. Calculate: add all the x's, add all the y's, then use Part 2.

Rx = x₁ + x₂ + …     Ry = y₁ + y₂ + …

Watch out: keep the signs. A vector pointing south has a negative y.

Part 5 · Which way? Finding direction10 min

Use the sizes of the components to get the angle from the east–west axis. Then use the signs to name it.

θ = tan⁻¹( |Ry| ÷ |Rx| )
RxRySay it as
++θ N of E
−+θ N of W
−−θ S of W
+−θ S of E

Quick checks: are you ready for Part 6?

Try these two after Part 5. They aren't graded. If you miss one, reread that part's worked example.

1. A vector is 10 m at 30° N of E. Which one gives its east (x) component?

2. A resultant has Rx = −4 km and Ry = +2 km. Which way does it point?

Part 6: Map Chester

⏱ about 45 min · 2 days

Now everything comes together. You'll draw a route through Chester's history to scale and track your displacement from Subaru Park after every stop.

6B · The Chester history route (30 min)

LegFrom → ToDisplacement
1Subaru Park → the Penn Landing site on the waterfront, where William Penn first came ashore in Pennsylvania in 16821.80 km at 34° N of E
2→ Widener University2.25 km at 77° N of E
3→ the former Crozer Theological Seminary in Upland, where Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. studied (1948–1951)1.97 km at 15° S of W

Stop positions are simplified for this activity, so they're close to the real locations but not exact. Use the map sheet in your packet. It already has the start, a north arrow and the scale (1 cm = 0.25 km). Leg 1 is done for you as a model, so you only work out legs 2 and 3.

For each leg: convert to cm → draw it → calculate x and y → add to your running totals → find displacement and direction. At the end, draw and measure one dashed arrow from Subaru Park to the last stop.

6C · Your own route to school (15 min)

  • Start somewhere you know: home, a relative's house, a park, a store or a bus stop. Finish at school.
  • 2 or 3 legs, with at least 1 diagonal leg.
  • Copy your map's scale bar. Measure it to find your scale. Example: if a 500 m bar is 2.5 cm long, then 1 cm = 0.2 km.
  • Fill in the 6C table and find your displacement from start to school.
◎

Close, not identical. Your measured and calculated answers should be within a few percent. Small differences come from pencil width and protractor reading. That's normal, and it's part of the point.

5

If you're stuck

⏱ 10-minute rule
  1. Draw it first. Most mistakes are about which side is which. A sketch with θ marked fixes most of them.
  2. Check degree mode. If sin(30) doesn't give 0.5, every trig answer will be wrong.
  3. Reread the worked example for that part. The problems follow the same steps.
  4. Still stuck after 10 minutes? Circle the problem. Write one sentence about what you tried. Move on, and message your teacher. Getting stuck and asking is part of the work.
