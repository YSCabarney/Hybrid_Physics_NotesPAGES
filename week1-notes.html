01

Scalars vs. Vectors

Every quantity in physics is either a scalar (just a size) or a vector (a size plus a direction). This one distinction is the key to the whole week.

SCALAR

Magnitude only

A number and a unit — no direction attached.

"The team ran 8.2 km total during the match."
VECTOR

Magnitude + direction

A number, a unit, and a direction — all three are required.

"The corner kick traveled 24 m toward the near post."
02

Distance vs. Displacement

Distance is the total ground covered (scalar). Displacement is how far you end up from where you started, in a straight line, with direction (vector). They're only equal if you move in a straight line and never turn back.

A player's actual run (gold, dashed) covers 65 m of ground. Her displacement (red arrow) — the straight line from where she started to where she stopped — is only 40 m, angled toward goal.
Why does the difference actually matter?

A GPS tracker vest reports both numbers for a reason. Distance tells you how much work an athlete's body did (fitness, fatigue). Displacement tells you how effective their movement was at actually changing position — a winger who covers 11 km but has a net displacement of almost zero over 90 minutes ran a lot without going anywhere in particular.

03

Speed vs. Velocity

Speed is how fast distance is covered (scalar). Velocity is how fast displacement changes — speed with a direction (vector).

AVERAGE SPEED
v = d / t
d = distance (m) · t = time (s) · v = speed (m/s)
AVERAGE VELOCITY
v = Δx / t
Δx = displacement (m, with direction) · v = velocity (m/s, with direction)

Worked Example

SCENARIO

A player receives the ball and drives 90 m in a straight line toward goal, reaching the box in 12 seconds. Find her average velocity.

  • GIVENdisplacement Δx = 90 m (toward goal), time t = 12 s
  • UNKNOWNaverage velocity, v
  • EQUATIONv = Δx / t
  • SUBSTITUTEv = 90 m / 12 s
  • SOLVEv = 7.5 m/s, toward goal (≈ 27 km/h)
05

Reading a Motion Graph

A position-time graph plots where an object is at each moment. For constant velocity, that graph is a straight line — and the slope of that line is the velocity.

A straight, rising line means constant velocity. The steeper the slope, the faster the object is moving.

Week 1 Cheat Sheet

  • Scalar = magnitude only. Vector = magnitude + direction.
  • Distance (scalar) = total ground covered. Displacement (vector) = straight-line change in position.
  • Speed (scalar) = d / t. Velocity (vector) = Δx / t, with a direction.
  • SI units: meters (m) for distance/displacement, seconds (s) for time, m/s for speed/velocity.
  • On a position-time graph, the slope of the line is the velocity — a straight line means constant velocity.
  • 1 m/s ≈ 3.6 km/h ≈ 2.24 mph.
